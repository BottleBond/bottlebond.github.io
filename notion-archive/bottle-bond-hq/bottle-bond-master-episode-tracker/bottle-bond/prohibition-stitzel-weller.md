### Episode Title: *The Legacy of Stitzel-Weller: From Prohibition to Bourbon Royalty*

---

### Introduction:

- Stitzel-Weller Distillery, founded by Julian P. "Pappy" Van Winkle, Sr. and Arthur Philip Stitzel in 1935, holds a legendary status in bourbon history.
- Opened just two years after the repeal of Prohibition on Derby Day—a date with deep cultural significance in Kentucky bourbon.
- Focus of this episode: how the distillery navigated Prohibition, emerged afterward, and became a beacon of wheated bourbon craftsmanship.

---

### The Impact of Prohibition (Overview):

- *Quick recap*: Prohibition (1920-1933) crippled the American whiskey industry.
- Many distilleries shut down, but a few, including W.L. Weller & Sons (Van Winkle's pre-Stitzel-Weller company), adapted by acquiring medicinal whiskey licenses.
- These loopholes allowed limited production, storage, and distribution of whiskey, mostly sold via prescription for medicinal purposes.

---

### Stitzel-Weller’s Role During Prohibition:

- **Medicinal Whiskey Production**: The medicinal whiskey license allowed W.L. Weller & Sons, led by Van Winkle, to keep operating at a reduced capacity during Prohibition. This became a key strategy for surviving the dry years.
- **Preservation of Aged Whiskey Stock**: Van Winkle carefully managed existing whiskey inventories during Prohibition, storing whiskey in warehouses and ensuring it was properly aged, which would later give Stitzel-Weller a major advantage post-Prohibition.
- **Strategic Positioning for Post-Prohibition**: Van Winkle maintained relationships with key industry figures and foresaw that Prohibition would eventually end, positioning himself and his partners to hit the ground running when it did.

---

### Founding of Stitzel-Weller (1935):

- **The Partnership**: Van Winkle, Arthur Philip Stitzel (a distiller), and Alex Farnsley (a financier) came together to found Stitzel-Weller Distillery.
    - Van Winkle brought his business acumen, marketing skill, and industry knowledge.
    - Stitzel provided technical expertise in distillation and quality control.
    - Farnsley contributed financial stability and business strategy.
- **Opening Day on Derby Day**: Stitzel-Weller officially opened its doors on May 4, 1935—Kentucky Derby Day—cementing its symbolic connection to Kentucky’s bourbon tradition.

---

### Post-Prohibition Innovation and Growth:

- **Wheated Bourbon**: Stitzel-Weller’s primary innovation was its focus on producing wheated bourbon, which used wheat instead of rye in the mash bill.
    - This produced a smoother, less spicy flavor profile compared to traditional rye-heavy bourbons, making Stitzel-Weller’s products stand out.
    - Old Fitzgerald, W.L. Weller, and Rebel Yell became flagship brands for the distillery, known for their distinctive, smooth taste.
- **Julian Van Winkle’s Marketing Genius**: Pappy Van Winkle’s business savvy included partnerships with retailers like Macy’s and exclusive private-label deals with major hotels like Hilton. These deals helped build Stitzel-Weller’s reputation beyond Kentucky.

---

### Expansion and the Bourbon Boom (1940s-1950s):

- **Rising Demand**: As the U.S. economy boomed post-WWII, demand for high-quality bourbon surged. Stitzel-Weller expanded production and increased capacity to meet growing consumer demand.
- **New Warehouses**: Additional warehouses were built to store aging bourbon, ensuring that the distillery could consistently supply well-matured products.
- **W.L. Weller and Old Fitzgerald**: By the 1950s, these brands were highly regarded in the whiskey world, with Old Fitzgerald in particular becoming a sought-after wheated bourbon.

---

### Corporate Changes and Decline (1970s-1990s):

- **1972 Norton Simon Acquisition**: Stitzel-Weller was acquired by the Norton Simon conglomerate. While bourbon production continued at high standards under master distiller Edwin Foote, the shift to corporate ownership signaled the beginning of changes in production methods and priorities.
- **Decline in the 1980s**: The overall decline in bourbon sales during the 1980s, as consumer tastes shifted to vodka and clear spirits, impacted Stitzel-Weller’s operations.
- **Closure in 1992**: Stitzel-Weller ceased production in 1992, a casualty of changing market forces and corporate restructuring.

---

### Stitzel-Weller’s Legacy:

- **The Birth of Pappy Van Winkle Bourbon**: Even after Stitzel-Weller closed, the legacy of its wheated bourbon lives on through the Van Winkle family’s continued production, now managed by Buffalo Trace Distillery. Pappy Van Winkle bourbon, made from Stitzel-Weller’s original wheated bourbon recipe, has become one of the most coveted whiskeys in the world.
- **Reopening in 2014**: Although distilling has not resumed, the Stitzel-Weller site was reopened to the public in 2014 by Diageo as part of the Bulleit Frontier Whiskey Experience on the Kentucky Bourbon Trail.
    - Today, it serves as the home to brands like Blade & Bow, I.W. Harper, and the Orphan Barrel series, honoring the distillery’s rich history.

---

### Conclusion:

- **Lasting Influence**: Stitzel-Weller’s innovative wheated bourbon recipe revolutionized the bourbon industry and continues to influence modern whiskey-making.
- **A Monument to Craftsmanship**: Though distilling no longer takes place on-site, Stitzel-Weller’s legacy endures, representing the resilience, craftsmanship, and passion that define Kentucky bourbon.
