**Part 1: The Rise of Schenley and Prohibition’s Opportunity**

- **Introduction to Schenley Distilleries**
    - Founded in 1920 by Lewis Rosenstiel, capitalizing on Prohibition’s unique landscape.
    - Initially a consolidation of small distilleries, later becoming a massive spirits empire.
- **Schenley’s Strategic Moves During Prohibition**
    - Obtaining a medicinal whiskey permit, one of only six companies granted this privilege.
    - Acquiring valuable stocks of pre-Prohibition whiskey and warehouses to dominate the medicinal spirits market.
    - Innovative business practices, such as strategically placing warehouses to maintain national distribution under Prohibition laws.
- **Rosenstiel’s Vision and Ruthlessness**
    - Building Schenley as a vertically integrated operation, controlling distilleries, cooperages, and distribution.
    - Aggressive tactics in the Prohibition market, competing fiercely against rivals like Frankfort Distillery and National Distillers.
- **Cultural Context**
    - The role of Schenley in maintaining whiskey’s relevance during Prohibition, ensuring its survival for the post-Repeal boom.

**Part 2: Schenley’s Golden Era and Post-Prohibition Impact**

- **Prohibition Repeal and Schenley’s Expansion**
    - Leveraging its massive inventory and distillery infrastructure post-1933.
    - Acquiring notable distilleries like Geo. T. Stagg (Ancient Age), J.W. Dant, and Cascade Hollow (Geo. A. Dickel).
- **1950s Overproduction and Industry Challenges**
    - Schenley’s miscalculation of demand during the Korean War, leading to massive overstocking of whiskey.
    - Rosenstiel’s lobbying success in extending the bonding period from 8 to 20 years, saving the company from bankruptcy.
- **Legacy in the Global Market**
    - Introducing key Canadian whisky brands (e.g., Canadian Club and Canadian O.F.C.).
    - Promoting bourbon overseas with efforts like the Bourbon Institute and I.W. Harper’s global distribution.
- **Decline and Dissolution**
    - Rosenstiel’s retirement in 1968 and the eventual sale to Glen Alden Corporation.
    - Schenley’s gradual dismantling, ending with its 1987 acquisition by United Distillers.
