**Part 1: Origins and the Prohibition Connection**

- **Introduction to Frankfort Distillery**
    - Established in 1902 near Frankfort, KY.
    - Founding members: John D. Hinde (President), R.A. Baker (VP), George Baker (Sec./Treasurer).
    - Key brands like "Old Crow" and "Paul Jones" before Prohibition.
- **Prohibition Era (1920-1933)**
    - Securing one of only six permits to sell medicinal whiskey.
    - Acquisition by Paul Jones & Co. in 1922 to leverage the medicinal whiskey license.
    - Operations during Prohibition: Contracting whiskey production to A. Ph. Stitzel Distillery.
- **Tasting Moment**
    - Introduce the 4 Roses pour. Highlight its connection to the Paul Jones legacy, which transitioned into Frankfort Distillery’s operations.

**Part 2: Post-Prohibition Expansion and the Role of American Medicinal Spirits**

- **Repeal of Prohibition (1933)**
    - Frankfort Distilleries acquires the old Stitzel plant and builds the Four Roses distillery in Shively.
    - Transition into a post-Prohibition whiskey boom, producing well-known brands like Antique, Paul Jones, and Four Roses.
- **American Medicinal Spirits (AMS)**
    - Origins as a centralized distributor of medicinal whiskey during Prohibition.
    - Key role in blending and distributing medicinal spirits for companies like Frankfort Distillery.
    - Growth and consolidation after Prohibition, eventually merging into National Distillers.
- **Historical Note**
    - The medicinal whiskey permit system and its impact on brands' survival.
- **Tasting Moment**
    - Reflect on 4 Roses’ evolution from a Prohibition staple to its modern legacy.
