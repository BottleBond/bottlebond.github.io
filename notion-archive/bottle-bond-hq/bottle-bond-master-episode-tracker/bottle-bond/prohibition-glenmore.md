### Episode Title: *Glenmore Distillery: A Bourbon Legacy from Owensboro to Today*

---

### Introduction:

- **Glenmore Distillery**, one of the most enduring names in Kentucky bourbon, traces its roots back to 1849.
- Originally founded as the R. Monarch Distillery, it became a key player in the bourbon industry after its acquisition by the Thompson family in 1901.
- In this episode, we'll explore Glenmore's history, including its role during Prohibition, its innovations, and its continued influence as part of the Sazerac family today.

---

### Early Beginnings and the Thompson Brothers:

- **R. Monarch Distillery Origins (1849)**: Glenmore started as the R. Monarch Distillery, producing notable bourbon brands like Kentucky Tavern.
- **Bankruptcy and Acquisition (1901)**: Facing financial struggles, the distillery entered bankruptcy in 1898, eventually being purchased by James Thompson and his brother Francis P. Thompson for $30,000.
    - **Rebranding as Glenmore Distillery**: The brothers renamed it Glenmore Distillery and began growing the company’s bourbon portfolio.
- **Kentucky Tavern and Old Thompson (1903-1904)**: Under the Thompson family’s leadership, Glenmore registered its first major brand, Kentucky Tavern, in 1903. This would become one of their flagship bourbons.
    - Old Thompson, another iconic brand, was introduced in 1904, showcasing the family’s intent to create high-quality bourbon that could compete on the national stage.

---

### Glenmore During Prohibition (1920-1933):

- **Concentration Warehouse & Medicinal Whiskey**: When Prohibition hit in 1920, Glenmore adapted by obtaining a federal permit to serve as a concentration warehouse.
    - Medicinal whiskey licenses allowed the distillery to continue producing and distributing whiskey legally for medicinal purposes during Prohibition, similar to other major distilleries of the time.
- **Leadership of Frank and James P. Thompson**: After James Thompson passed away in 1924, his sons Frank B. Thompson and James P. Thompson took the reins.
    - Col. Frank B. Thompson, a central figure in the company’s post-Prohibition revival, became chairman and president, steering the distillery through the complex regulatory environment and positioning it for post-Prohibition success.

---

### Post-Prohibition Revival and Expansion:

- **Post-Prohibition Boom**: When Prohibition ended in 1933, Glenmore was well-positioned to immediately restart bourbon production.
    - Kentucky Tavern, a key brand before Prohibition, resumed production, quickly becoming one of the distillery’s best-selling bourbons.
- **Acquisition of Yellowstone Bourbon (1944)**: Glenmore expanded its portfolio in 1944 by purchasing the Yellowstone Bourbon brand from the Taylor & Williams Distillery in Louisville.
    - Yellowstone became a major addition to the Glenmore lineup, further solidifying the distillery’s influence in the bourbon world.

---

### Glenmore’s Innovations and Expansions in the Mid-20th Century:

- **Diversification of Spirits (1950s-1960s)**: Glenmore diversified beyond bourbon, introducing new products such as Glenmore Vodka (1955) and Glenmore Gin (1959). These moves marked the distillery’s shift into broader markets.
    - **Two-Millionth Barrel of Bourbon (1955)**: A major milestone was reached in 1955 when Glenmore barreled its two-millionth barrel of bourbon.
    - **Innovative Marketing**: In 1959, Glenmore became the first distillery to break a long-standing industry taboo by employing women as part of its spirits sales team, a progressive move in an industry dominated by men.
- **Imported Spirits**: Glenmore followed industry trends by marketing imported whiskies and cordials through its subsidiaries such as Mr. Boston, Foreign Vintages, and Viking Distillery.

---

### Corporate Changes and the End of the Thompson Era:

- **Acquisition by Guinness (1991)**: After nearly a century of control by the Thompson family, Glenmore was acquired by Guinness in 1991.
    - Guinness merged Glenmore with Schenley Industries, forming United Distillers, but the changes in ownership led to shifts in production and management.
- **Sale to Barton Brands (1995)**: Glenmore changed hands again when Barton Brands purchased the distillery in 1995, continuing production under the Barton umbrella.

---

### Glenmore Under Sazerac Ownership (2009-Present):

- **Sazerac Acquisition (2009)**: In 2009, Glenmore was acquired by Sazerac as part of a $334 million deal that also included Barton Distillery and other brands from Constellation Brands.
    - **Focus on Bottling**: Though Glenmore had largely shifted to bottling operations by this point, Sazerac maintained the historic Glenmore Distillery as part of its bourbon portfolio.
- **Resumption of Distilling (2016)**: In 2016, Glenmore reintroduced distillation, though initially focusing on rum production. This marked the return of distilling to the historic site after decades of being used primarily for bottling and storage.

---

### Glenmore’s Enduring Legacy:

- **Kentucky Tavern and Yellowstone**: Even though Glenmore has undergone multiple ownership changes, iconic brands like Kentucky Tavern and Yellowstone continue to be produced, carrying forward the distillery’s rich history.
- **Innovation in Bourbon and Beyond**: From pioneering the use of women in spirits sales to diversifying its product range, Glenmore’s influence extended beyond traditional bourbon-making.
- **Part of the Sazerac Bourbon Family**: Today, Glenmore is part of the Sazerac family, standing alongside other bourbon giants. Though its focus has evolved, Glenmore remains a crucial part of Owensboro’s bourbon legacy and a key player in Kentucky’s storied distilling history.

---

### Conclusion:

- **Resilience and Innovation**: Glenmore’s ability to adapt and thrive during Prohibition, its expansion into other spirits markets, and its enduring bourbon brands are testaments to the Thompson family’s vision and the distillery’s resilience.
- **A Legacy Reborn**: Now part of the Sazerac empire, Glenmore continues to evolve while preserving its roots, standing as a symbol of Kentucky’s bourbon heritage.
