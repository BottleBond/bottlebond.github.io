# Bourbon 101: A Beginner's Guide to America's Native Spirit

So you want to learn about bourbon? You've come to the right place. Whether you've just had your first sip or you're looking to move beyond the well drinks, this guide will give you the foundation you need.

## What Makes Bourbon... Bourbon?

Bourbon isn't just any whiskey. By law, it must meet these requirements:

1. **Made in the United States** -- while Kentucky produces 95% of the world's bourbon, it can legally be made anywhere in the U.S.
2. **At least 51% corn** in the mash bill (grain recipe)
3. **Aged in new, charred oak barrels** -- this is what gives bourbon its signature vanilla, caramel, and oak flavors
4. **Distilled to no more than 160 proof** (80% ABV)
5. **Entered into the barrel at no more than 125 proof** (62.5% ABV)
6. **Bottled at 80 proof or higher** (40% ABV minimum)

Notice what's *not* on this list: there's no minimum aging requirement for bourbon (though "straight bourbon" must be aged at least two years).

## The Three Grains

Most bourbon mash bills use three grains:

- **Corn** (51-80%): Provides sweetness and body
- **Rye** (or wheat): The "flavoring grain" -- rye adds spice, wheat adds softness
- **Malted barley** (5-15%): Contains enzymes that convert starches to fermentable sugars

### High-Rye vs. Wheated Bourbon

This is one of the first forks in the road for new bourbon drinkers:

- **High-rye bourbons** (like Bulleit, Four Roses Single Barrel) tend to be spicier with pepper and cinnamon notes
- **Wheated bourbons** (like Maker's Mark, Weller) are typically softer, sweeter, with more honey and bread notes

## How to Taste Bourbon

### Step 1: Look
Hold your glass up to the light. Note the color -- darker generally means older or more barrel influence. Swirl and watch the "legs" run down the glass.

### Step 2: Nose
This is where most of the flavor actually lives. Hold the glass just below your nose and breathe in gently. Don't stick your nose in the glass -- the alcohol fumes will overwhelm everything. Try nosing with your mouth slightly open.

### Step 3: Taste
Take a small sip and let it coat your tongue. Notice how the flavor evolves -- the initial taste (the "entry"), the middle, and the finish. Add a few drops of water and taste again; water opens up different flavors.

### Step 4: Note
Write down what you taste. There are no wrong answers. Common bourbon flavors include vanilla, caramel, oak, cinnamon, honey, toffee, leather, tobacco, and dried fruit.

## Your First Five Bottles

If you're building your first bourbon shelf, start here:

1. **Buffalo Trace** -- approachable, well-balanced, great value
2. **Maker's Mark** -- smooth wheated bourbon, excellent for beginners
3. **Wild Turkey 101** -- a bit more kick at 101 proof, incredible flavor for the price
4. **Woodford Reserve** -- refined, complex, great for sipping neat
5. **Elijah Craig Small Batch** -- a step up in age and complexity

## Next Steps

Once you've explored the basics, you're ready to start branching out. Try comparing:
- Different proof points (80 vs 100 vs barrel proof)
- Different ages (4-year vs 8-year vs 12-year)
- Different mash bills (high-rye vs wheated vs high-corn)

Welcome to the journey. It only gets more interesting from here.
